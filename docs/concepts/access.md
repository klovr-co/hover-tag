# Your own Tag

Your Tag is your personal agent in Slack. You ask it to help with your work,
using the conversation and the tools and accounts you've made available to
it. Your teammates can bring their own Tags, too.

## Bring your agent into the conversation

Work already happens in conversations: someone shares a plan, a teammate
raises a concern, and you agree on what to do next. Your Tag can help right
there. You don't have to carry the discussion into a separate AI chat and
bring the answer back.

> **Jules:** I’ll finish the FAQ by Tuesday.
>
> **Maya:** I’ll test signup on Wednesday. Support still needs a briefing.
>
> **Jules:** @Jules's Tag pull this into a launch checklist with owners.
>
> **Jules's Tag:** Here’s the shared checklist:
> - **Jules:** Finish the FAQ by Tuesday.
> - **Maya:** Test signup on Wednesday.
> - **Unassigned:** Brief support before launch. Who can take this?
>
> **Maya:** I'll brief support. We're also missing the go/no-go review. Jules, can you own that?

Jules directs Jules's Tag. Maya helps shape the work without having to use
Jules's agent.

Your requests, Tag's posted updates, and its results stay together in the
thread. Teammates can follow what you asked for and build on the result.

## You decide what to ask it

Only you can ask your Tag to work at first. It uses the access available to
your agent, which may include your files and connected accounts. Bringing it
into a channel doesn't give everyone there permission to direct it.

You can choose to let teammates request work from your Tag. They would be
using the same agent and its available accounts and tools; Tag doesn't give
each person a separate set of permissions. Sharing that access is a choice
you make, separate from sharing the results of your work.

## Personal doesn't mean hidden

Your Tag belongs to you, but requests and replies in a Slack channel are
visible to people who can access that channel. Choose where you ask based
on who should see the conversation and its results.

Tag can use the thread context it receives, along with available integrations.
It doesn't automatically know every conversation or have access to every
account. See [What Tag knows](what-tag-knows.md) and
[Adding integrations](adding-integrations.md) for more.

## Where it works

Tag runs on the computer hosting your agent. The files and accounts it can
use depend on that agent's permissions, the local account, and connected
tools. Its workspace folder is a starting place, not a security sandbox;
keeping Tag owner-only doesn't isolate the agent from that computer.

Before letting others use your Tag, use a dedicated computer or isolated
local account with only the access needed for the work. The
[security policy](../../SECURITY.md) explains the technical limits.
